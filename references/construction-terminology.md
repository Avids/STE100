# Construction terminology policy

Treat established construction terminology as permitted technical terminology when it is used with its recognized industry meaning.

## Default rule

Do not replace or flag a construction term solely because it is absent from ordinary STE vocabulary.

Permit standard construction, electrical, fire alarm, security, communications, mechanical-coordination, controls, civil, architectural, commissioning, testing, estimating, scheduling, contract-administration, and site-logistics terminology as technical nouns or technical verbs when appropriate.

Examples include terms such as:
- conduit, raceway, cable tray, junction box, pull box, panelboard, switchboard, transformer, disconnect, breaker, feeder, branch circuit, grounding conductor
- fire alarm, annunciator, strobe, horn, speaker, relay, module, device, loop, riser
- rough-in, fit-out, backfeed, energize, de-energize, terminate, megger, core drill, firestop, sleeve, fish, pull, label, tag, commission
- RFI, submittal, shop drawing, site instruction, change order, field condition, deficiency, punch list, shutdown, outage, tie-in, access, hoarding

These examples are not exhaustive.

## Conditions

A construction term is acceptable when:
1. It has a recognized technical meaning in the applicable trade or project context.
2. The user or source uses it consistently, or it is the normal industry term for the intended concept/process.
3. Replacing it with a general word could reduce precision or change scope.
4. It is used in the correct grammatical role.

## Consistency

Use one term for one item or process. Do not alternate between near-synonyms such as `panel`, `panelboard`, and `distribution panel` if they refer to the same specific item, unless the source documents require those distinct terms.

Preserve project-defined terminology even when another construction term is more common.

## Construction verbs

Permit established construction process verbs as technical verbs when they accurately describe the work. Keep verb forms simple and direct. For example, prefer `Install the conduit` to `The conduit installation is to be carried out`.

Do not convert a noun into a verb only because site slang does so. Use the recognized technical verb form when available.

## Contract and design responsibility

STE editing must not change responsibility or entitlement. Do not turn a recommendation into a requirement, a target into a guarantee, or a consultant/owner responsibility into a contractor responsibility.

Preserve conditional language such as `subject to`, `contingent upon`, `after approval`, `when access is available`, and similar dependency language when it is technically or contractually necessary. Simplify the sentence structure without deleting the dependency.
